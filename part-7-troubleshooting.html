Part 7: Troubleshooting Log

Series: Enterprise Multi-Site Campus Network on GNS3. Part 7 of 8.

Most of what I learned came from things that did not work. Each entry has the symptom, what I first thought, the real cause and the fix.

My troubleshooting order

  1. Layer 2 first when HSRP and OSPF flap together: check logs for SPANTREE and err-disable messages, then root bridge placement, then guard placement.
  2. OSPF adjacency: interface status and subnet, router ID, area and wildcard, network type and timers, then neighbour state. INIT usually means Hello is one-way; EXSTART or LOADING suggests an MTU mismatch or duplicate router ID.
  3. HSRP: check the inter-distribution trunk, SVI state, then compare group, virtual IP and priority on both switches with show standby brief.
  4. Data plane: check the typed addresses first, then show ip route for the destination, then CEF.

Trunk pruning question

Pruning trunks to only floor VLANs looked like a fix for unicast flooding, but staff who work on another floor would lose access. The modern answer is dynamic VLAN assignment with 802.1X, RADIUS and identity-based policy, which I list on the roadmap.
