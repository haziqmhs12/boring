I use /30 for transit links because /31 is the usual choice on point-to-point links but not every device supports it, and my first simulator did not.

Sparse allocation

Instead of packing sites tightly, I leave a full block free between sites. If a branch grows, it can expand into its own gap without overlapping the next site or rewriting summary routes.

Why separate loopback, management and transit ranges

Early on I combined these into one subnet because I did not understand the differences. They do different jobs: a loopback is a virtual interface that stays up even when a physical link fails, and is used as the OSPF router ID and as tunnel endpoint. An out-of-band management network carries SSH and SNMP to devices. Transit links only connect routers. Separating them keeps management reachable when data links fail and keeps routing tables clean.

Sizing subnets

I size each VLAN with a simple rule:

Total IPs = current devices x 1.5 x 1.2

The 1.5 factor covers overlapping DHCP leases, for example when a device leaves but keeps its lease for hours. The 1.2 factor adds 20 percent growth. Examples from the design:

Guest Wi-Fi follows turnover instead of headcount, so for guest networks I also add the arrival rate and the DHCP lease time to the count of active leases. A busy store can exceed 254 shoppers over a few hours, so the branch guest VLAN is a /23.
